Appointment Booking Setup

Never Lose Another Lead to "I'll Call You Back"

What This Guide Covers

This guide shows you how to set up an appointment booking system that lets prospects schedule time with you directly — no phone tag, no back-and-forth emails, no lost leads. By the end, you'll have a booking system that works 24/7 and integrates seamlessly with your calendar.

Part 1: Why Appointment Booking Matters

The Problem with Manual Scheduling

Before:

  • Prospect fills out form
  • You email them 3 options
  • They reply with different times
  • You check your calendar
  • They don't respond
  • Lead goes cold

After:

  • Prospect visits your booking page
  • They see your real availability
  • They pick a time that works
  • Meeting appears on both calendars
  • Confirmation email sent automatically
  • Reminder sent before the call
Result: More booked calls, less admin work, happier prospects.

Part 2: Setting Up Your Booking Calendar

Step 1: Connect Your Calendar

What you need:

  • Google Calendar, Outlook, or iCloud calendar
  • 10 minutes to set up the connection

How to connect:

  1. Go to Settings → Calendar Integration
  2. Click "Connect Calendar"
  3. Sign in to your calendar provider
  4. Grant permission to read/write events
  5. Select which calendar to use for bookings

Step 2: Set Your Availability

Availability settings:

  • Working hours: When are you actually available? (e.g., 9 AM - 5 PM)
  • Working days: Which days? (Monday-Friday? Include Saturday?)
  • Time zone: Set to YOUR time zone, system handles conversion
  • Buffer time: Add 15 minutes between calls

Step 3: Create Appointment Types

Different meetings need different setups:

Type 1: Discovery Call (15 minutes)

  • Purpose: Quick qualification
  • When to use: First contact, cold leads
  • Buffer: 15 minutes after

Type 2: Strategy Session (30 minutes)

  • Purpose: Deep dive, proposal discussion
  • When to use: Qualified leads, existing clients
  • Buffer: 15 minutes after

Type 3: Onboarding Call (45 minutes)

  • Purpose: New client kickoff
  • When to use: After sale is made
  • Buffer: 30 minutes after

Part 3: Customizing Your Booking Page

Step 4: Design Your Booking Page

Required elements:

1. Headline: Clear promise

  • Good: "Book Your Free Strategy Session"
  • Bad: "Schedule a Meeting"

2. Subheadline: What happens next

  • Good: "In 30 minutes, we'll map out a plan to [solve their problem]"
  • Bad: "Pick a time below"

3. Your photo: Build trust

  • Professional headshot
  • Friendly expression
  • Consistent with your brand

Step 5: Add Qualifying Questions

Filter out time-wasters before they book:

Essential questions to ask:

  1. "What's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?"
    • Purpose: Understand their pain
    • Required: Yes
  2. "What made you decide to book this call now?"
    • Purpose: Gauge urgency
    • Required: Yes
  3. "Have you worked with a [your service type] before?"
    • Purpose: Set expectations
    • Required: No
Pro tip: Don't ask too many questions. 3-4 max or you'll lose bookings.

Step 6: Confirmation and Reminder Settings

Reduce no-shows with automatic follow-up:

Confirmation email (immediate):

  • Subject: "You're booked! [Date] at [Time]"
  • Include: Date, time, your photo, what to prepare
  • Add to calendar button (Google, Outlook, Apple)
  • Reschedule link

Reminder email (24 hours before):

  • Subject: "Tomorrow: Your strategy session"
  • Include: Quick agenda reminder
  • Join link (if video call)

Reminder SMS (2 hours before):

  • "Hi [Name], reminder: our call is at [Time]. Here's the link: [URL]"
  • Only if they provided phone number

Part 4: Integration and Automation

Step 7: Connect to Your CRM

Every booking should trigger your follow-up system:

Automatic actions on new booking:

  1. Create/update contact in CRM
  2. Tag as "Call Booked"
  3. Move to "Scheduled Call" pipeline stage
  4. Add to "Upcoming Calls" smart list
  5. Send notification to you (email/SMS)

Step 8: Set Up Video Calls

Option 1: Built-in video

  • Pros: Simple, no extra accounts
  • Cons: Less familiar to prospects

Option 2: Zoom integration

  • Pros: Everyone knows Zoom
  • Cons: Requires Zoom account

Option 3: Google Meet

  • Pros: No software download needed
  • Cons: 60-minute limit on free tier

Part 5: Advanced Booking Strategies

Step 9: Create Urgency with Limited Slots

Tactics that work:

  1. Show limited availability — "Only 3 spots left this week"
  2. Offer specific time windows — "I take calls Tuesday and Thursday mornings"
  3. Seasonal availability — "Booking 2 weeks out due to demand"

Step 10: Reduce No-Shows

Pre-call engagement:

  1. Immediate value: Send a quick video after booking
  2. Preparation email: 48 hours before, send "To get the most from our call, please think about..."
  3. Confirmation required: Ask them to reply "CONFIRM" to the reminder

Part 6: Tracking and Optimization

Step 11: Monitor Your Metrics

Metric Target What It Tells You
Booking rate >20% How compelling your offer is
Show-up rate >70% Quality of your reminders
Conversion rate >30% How well you sell on the call
Time to book <7 days Urgency of your prospects

Step 12: A/B Test Your Booking Page

Elements to test:

  • Headline: Problem-focused vs. outcome-focused
  • Photo: Professional vs. casual
  • Length: Short form vs. detailed form
  • CTA button: "Book Now" vs. "Schedule My Call"
  • Social proof: Testimonial vs. stats

Quick Reference: Booking Setup Checklist

Initial Setup

  • Calendar connected
  • Working hours set
  • Buffer time configured
  • Appointment types created
  • Booking page customized
  • Qualifying questions added
  • Confirmation email written
  • Reminder emails scheduled
  • CRM integration active
  • Video call connected (if needed)

Optimization

  • Test booking flow yourself
  • Review metrics weekly
  • A/B test one element monthly
  • Update availability seasonally
  • Refresh testimonials quarterly

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Too many appointment types — Confuses prospects, keep it to 3-4
  2. No buffer time — You'll burn out or run late
  3. Weak confirmation emails — Missed opportunity to set expectations
  4. Ignoring no-shows — Track and optimize this metric
  5. Set and forget — Review and update quarterly
